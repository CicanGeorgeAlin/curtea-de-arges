# CURTEA DE ARGEȘ — MASTER RESEARCH REGISTER

**Project:** Curtea de Argeș — Arhiva Vie  
**Creator and curator:** Cican George Alin  
**Purpose:** a long-term, source-controlled digital historical archive of Curtea de Argeș.

## Research standard

No important historical claim is treated as established merely because it is repeated online. Important claims are to be checked against primary, institutional and academic evidence wherever accessible.

## Evidence vocabulary

- **VERIFIED** — strong supporting evidence, preferably from primary or multiple independent authoritative sources.
- **SUPPORTED** — credible institutional or academic evidence, with further corroboration desirable.
- **DISPUTED** — credible sources disagree.
- **TRADITION** — later/local tradition; never silently presented as established fact.
- **UNKNOWN** — currently insufficient evidence.

## Initial source register

### National Archaeological Repertory (RAN)
The official RAN record identifies the historic urban site of Curtea de Argeș as **RAN 13631.11 / LMI AG-II-s-A-13645**. It describes a medieval urban settlement dated broadly to the **13th–19th centuries** and gives a detailed protected-area description.

Source: https://ran.cimec.ro/sel.asp?descript=curtea-de-arges-municipiul-curtea-de-arges-arges-situl-urban-orasul-istoric-curtea-de-arges-de-la-curtea-de-arges-cod-sit-ran-13631.11

### Argeș Monastery complex
RAN **13631.10 / LMI AG-II-a-A-13628**. The record identifies medieval and modern components, including the church dated **1512–1517**, chapel dated **1885**, and episcopal palace dated **1885**.

Source: https://ran.cimec.ro/sel.asp?descript=curtea-de-arges-municipiul-curtea-de-arges-arges-ansamblul-manastirii-argesului-de-la-curtea-de-arges-cod-sit-ran-13631.10&lang=EN

### Municipal historical study
The municipal historical study provides an initial chronology including Seneslau, Basarab I, the 1330 campaign, the 1336 documentary reference, Biserica Domnească, the 1359 metropolitan see, the 1369 document, the move of the princely court, the 1510 name reference and the 1512–1517 episcopal church.

Source: https://www.primariacurteadearges.ro/portal/arges/cda/portalcda.nsf/AllByUNID/5768583B29F80D30C2258C350032BF6E/%24FILE/4.%20Studiu%20istoric.pdf

### Municipal cultural material
The municipality describes its local history exhibition as presenting documentary and archaeological material on the history of Curtea de Argeș, including facsimile documents, representations of rulers, replicas of princely objects, numismatics, medals, decorations and archaeological material.

Source: https://www.primariacurteadearges.ro/dm_ag_curteadearges/portalcda.nsf/AllByUNID/invatamant-si-cultura-00005aaa

## Master research programme

1. Establish the earliest geological, palaeoenvironmental and human evidence relevant to the territory.
2. Separate prehistoric, ancient, early-medieval and medieval evidence.
3. Reconcile every important medieval date across independent sources.
4. Inventory every entry in the national and local monument registers.
5. Build a monument/place database with coordinates, names, alternate names, periods, status and sources.
6. Catalogue archaeological excavations, finds and publications.
7. Reconstruct the medieval princely centre and urban morphology.
8. Document churches, monasteries, cemeteries, memorials, historic houses and disappeared structures.
9. Research rulers, clergy, artists, builders, craftspeople, scholars and other historically documented people.
10. Research administration, law, economy, crafts, trade, education, health, transport, railway and everyday life.
11. Research royal history and the 19th–20th century transformation of the city.
12. Research both World Wars, the communist period and the post-1989 period.
13. Collect historical maps, photographs, postcards and visual records with rights/attribution metadata.
14. Record oral history and local tradition separately from documentary evidence.
15. Build the historical map layer.
16. Maintain the archive continuously as new evidence becomes accessible.

## Future data model

Each significant record should eventually support:

- title
- record type
- exact/approximate date
- historical period
- location
- latitude / longitude
- alternate names
- people/entities
- events
- description
- evidence status
- primary sources
- institutional sources
- academic bibliography
- archaeological references
- image references
- rights/licensing
- related records
- last verification date
- research notes

## Founding principle

> Every generation adds something. Nothing important is allowed to disappear.

This archive is designed to grow for decades.
