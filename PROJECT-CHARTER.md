# PROJECT CHARTER — HISTORICAL FIRST

Project: **Curtea de Argeș — Arhiva Vie**  
Founder, creator and curator: **Cican George Alin**

## The foundation

The primary purpose of this project is to build the most complete, carefully sourced and continuously expandable digital historical archive of Curtea de Argeș that can be created from accessible evidence.

The website is not being built primarily as a visual tourism product.

**History comes first. The archive comes first. Accuracy comes first.**

The public website is the first interface to a deeper research system.

## What “complete” means

The project aims for **100% completeness as an objective**, while recognizing that no historical archive can honestly claim to possess literally every surviving piece of evidence.

Therefore, “100%” means:

- leave no historical period unexplored;
- leave no category of evidence deliberately omitted;
- identify every relevant monument and archaeological site that can be documented;
- identify people, institutions, rulers, clergy, builders, artists, craftspeople, scholars and other documented actors;
- identify major and minor events;
- document streets, neighbourhoods, buildings, cemeteries, memorials, religious sites, civic buildings, infrastructure and places that have disappeared;
- investigate archaeology, documentary history, architecture, religion, administration, economy, crafts, trade, education, health, transport, warfare, ordinary life and cultural memory;
- preserve historical names and variants;
- distinguish evidence from interpretation and tradition;
- record uncertainty rather than inventing certainty;
- continuously search for new primary sources, archival material, scholarly work, museum records, maps, photographs and other evidence;
- make the source trail visible enough that important claims can be checked.

A record can therefore be **complete in research coverage while still containing unknowns**. Unknown is a valid historical result.

## Research before presentation

The sequence is intentional:

**RESEARCH → SOURCE CONTROL → STRUCTURED ARCHIVE → HISTORICAL SYNTHESIS → WEBSITE → MAP → APP**

The visual design must never drive the historical interpretation.

## Source hierarchy

When sources conflict, investigate rather than averaging them.

Priority normally follows:

1. primary documents and archaeological evidence;
2. official archaeological and heritage registers;
3. archival and museum collections;
4. scholarly books, editions and peer-reviewed research;
5. institutional historical studies;
6. specialist secondary sources;
7. reputable local historical material;
8. oral history and tradition, clearly identified as such.

A lower-level source may still contain unique information and should not be discarded simply because it is local. It should be classified and checked.

## The archive as a system

Every important historical fact should eventually be represented as a structured record connected to:

- date or date range;
- place;
- people/entities;
- event;
- historical period;
- evidence status;
- source(s);
- bibliography;
- archaeological reference where applicable;
- image/document provenance;
- rights/licensing;
- related records;
- research notes;
- last verification date.

This structure is what will later allow the same historical foundation to power the website, interactive map and mobile application without rebuilding the research.

## Website first

The website is the first public expression of the archive.

Its purpose is to make the accumulated research understandable, searchable and navigable.

The first major development period should therefore be devoted to the historical database and website rather than rushing into an application.

## Future map and application

Only after the historical foundation is sufficiently mature should the project become location-aware.

Then the same records can support:

- historical map layers;
- precise heritage/place locations;
- walking routes;
- monument and archaeological-site discovery;
- historical overlays;
- location-based stories;
- visitor trails;
- personal photographs attached to existing historical records;
- an eventual mobile application.

The app is not a separate history project. It is a future interface to the same archive.

## Long-term principle

The project should be able to grow for decades.

New evidence must be added without destroying previous research history. Corrections should be transparent. New discoveries should have somewhere to go.

The goal is not simply to make a website that says many things about Curtea de Argeș.

The goal is to build a durable digital memory of the city.

**Every generation adds something. Nothing important is allowed to disappear.**
