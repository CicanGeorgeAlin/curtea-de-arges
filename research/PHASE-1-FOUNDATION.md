# Curtea de Argeș — Phase 1 Research Foundation

Creator and curator: **Cican George Alin**

Status: working research record — 21 September 2026

## Objective

Build a source-controlled historical archive of Curtea de Argeș that can grow for decades. The archive is designed as a living digital record, not a tourism brochure.

The first production phase establishes the evidence base, source register, chronology framework and heritage inventory.

## Research rules

1. A repeated statement is not automatically an established fact.
2. Primary documents, archaeological records, institutional registers and academic research receive priority.
3. Every important historical assertion should carry a source trail.
4. Dating must preserve the precision actually supported by the evidence: exact year, range, century, approximate date, or unknown.
5. Separate **VERIFIED**, **SUPPORTED**, **DISPUTED**, **TRADITION**, and **UNKNOWN**.
6. Legends and later traditions are preserved because they are part of the city's cultural memory, but they are not presented as documentary fact.
7. Historic names, modern names and variant spellings should be retained rather than silently normalized away.
8. Lost, altered, relocated and surviving structures should be tracked separately.
9. Images require provenance and rights metadata before publication.
10. The archive must remain expandable: new evidence can revise an entry without erasing the research history.

## Initial evidence base

### 1. National Archaeological Repertory (RAN)

The RAN record for the historic urban site identifies **RAN 13631.11** and LMI **AG-II-s-A-13645**, with a medieval urban settlement dated broadly to the 13th–19th centuries. The record was last modified in 2023 and the RAN database was updated in September 2026.

### 2. RAN — Argeș Monastery complex

The RAN record identifies **RAN 13631.10**, LMI **AG-II-a-A-13628**, at Bd. Basarabilor 1. Its listed components include the medieval church dated 1512–1517, a chapel dated 1885 and an episcopal palace dated 1885.

### 3. RAN — Curtea Domnească

RAN **13631.07**, LMI **AG-II-a-A-13647**, records the medieval princely court at Str. Negru Vodă 2. The record lists archaeological components including the Basarab I princely-house ruins (around 1345), the princely church, enclosure structures and later elements.

### 4. RAN — Sân Nicoară

RAN **13631.05**, LMI **AG-II-m-A-13655.01**, records the ruins of Sân Nicoară at Str. Sân Nicoară 1. The RAN record describes the site as a religious building and places it on a hill overlooking the surrounding valleys.

### 5. Museum evidence

The Municipal Museum of Curtea de Argeș states that its collections cover archaeology, history, numismatics, ethnography and art, including material from the Neolithic, Dacian, Roman, Byzantine and medieval periods. This makes the museum an essential institutional research partner/source for the archive.

### 6. UNESCO Tentative List

The UNESCO World Heritage Centre has a 1991 Tentative List entry for the Byzantine and post-Byzantine churches of Curtea de Argeș. It describes the city as a former capital of Wallachia and identifies the medieval princely court, Saint Nicholas Princely Church, Sân Nicoară ruins and the 1512–1517 monastery church as major heritage elements.

This UNESCO page is treated as an institutional historical description, not as proof of every date or interpretation appearing elsewhere.

## Immediate production programme

- Build the master source register.
- Build the monument/heritage inventory from the LMI and RAN.
- Reconcile RAN, LMI, municipal and museum terminology.
- Build a chronological evidence table.
- Add archaeological records before attempting a polished narrative of prehistory.
- Separate the medieval princely-centre story from later royal and modern transformations.
- Add people, institutions, buildings, streets, cemeteries, memorials and disappeared structures as linked records.
- Add historical maps and photographs only with provenance/rights fields.
- Add a revision log so later discoveries can correct earlier entries transparently.

## Current caution

The archive must not reduce the city's history to the famous medieval monuments. The research programme explicitly includes archaeological evidence, ordinary urban life, religious communities, education, health, crafts, trade, transport, war, political change, industrial and modern development, the communist period, post-1989 change, and living memory.
