# Curtea de Argeș — Archaeology Research Pass 01

Project: **Curtea de Argeș — Arhiva Vie**  
Creator and curator: **Cican George Alin**  
Research date: **21 September 2026**

## Purpose

This pass begins the archaeological layer of the archive. The objective is not to write a smooth historical narrative yet, but to identify archaeological records, excavations, reports and research questions that must become linked archive records.

## Findings

### A. Historic urban site — RAN 13631.11

The National Archaeological Repertory (RAN) identifies the protected historic urban site as **RAN 13631.11 / LMI AG-II-s-A-13645**. The RAN record describes an urban settlement of the medieval period, broadly **13th–19th centuries**, and gives a detailed protected-area perimeter. The record was updated in 2026.

**Status:** VERIFIED as an official archaeological/heritage register entry.

**Research implication:** this record is a container for a very large urban archaeological and historical landscape. It must not be treated as a single monument. The streets, plots, buildings, religious sites, cemeteries and archaeological points inside it need separate linked records.

### B. Curtea Domnească — RAN 13631.07

RAN records the princely court at **Str. Negru Vodă 2** as a medieval civil settlement. Its components include a princely court dated approximately **1345**, the Domnească Church dated **1351–1370**, and additional medieval and modern components.

The RAN description states that the Domnească Church is associated with Basarab I and records completion in 1352, while the LMI/RAN dating field gives 1351–1370.

**Status:** VERIFIED for the register data; individual historical interpretations require source-by-source attribution.

A 1967 archaeological study by Nicolae Constantinescu is preserved in the Digital Library of Romania. It describes the Curtea Domnească complex as a major archaeological and architectural research subject and discusses discoveries from earlier excavations, including evidence of an earlier chapel beneath the present Domnească Church.

**Source:** Nicolae Constantinescu, “Cercetarea arheologică de la Curtea Domnească din Argeș, 1967”, *Argesis*, 1968.

### C. Sân Nicoară — RAN 13631.05

RAN records the Sân Nicoară site at **Str. Sân Nicoară nr. 1**. The site has been the subject of archaeological research and architectural/material studies.

The 2011 research report in the *Cronica Cercetărilor Arheologice* records an archaeological investigation intended to clarify:
- the age of the church and tower;
- whether church and tower were built together;
- the characteristics and possible function of the tower;
- the extent and character of the surrounding site;
- whether an enclosure wall or fortification existed on the hill;
- the relationship between Sân Nicoară, the Domnească Church and the medieval city.

The report also records stratigraphic observations from the tower area and notes that later 19th- and 20th-century modifications affected the present ground levels.

**Status:** VERIFIED that archaeological research addressed these questions. The individual interpretations must be recorded with their authors and evidence.

### D. Drujești — RAN 13631.02

A previously underrepresented archaeological record has been identified: **Biserica și necropola medievală de la Curtea de Argeș — Drujești**, RAN **13631.02**.

RAN records:
- a medieval church dated from the **late 16th century to the second half of the 18th century**;
- a medieval cemetery dated to the **16th–17th centuries**;
- systematic archaeological research in **1987** by the Muzeul Județean Argeș, associated with archaeologist Spiridon Cristocea.

**Status:** VERIFIED as an archaeological register entry and verified research record.

**Research implication:** Drujești demonstrates why the archive cannot be reduced to the famous princely monuments. Religious and funerary archaeology outside the central court complex must be included in the city database.

## Important source discovery

The Digital Library of Romania contains older specialist literature on Curtea de Argeș, including:
- archaeological studies of the Domnească Court;
- studies of the Domnească Church;
- historical monument publications from the former Commission of Historic Monuments;
- anthropological research on human remains found during excavations at the Domnească Church.

These older publications are particularly important because they preserve observations, plans, photographs and interpretations that may not exist in modern summaries.

## Research rule

An archaeological report does not automatically make every interpretation in the report “fact”. The archive will preserve:

**site evidence → report → author/institution → interpretation → later confirmation or disagreement**

This will allow the archive to show how knowledge about Curtea de Argeș was produced and revised over time.

## Next archaeological targets

1. Build the complete RAN list for the administrative territory of Curtea de Argeș.
2. Retrieve every available *Cronica Cercetărilor Arheologice* report connected to the city.
3. Retrieve older archaeological publications from the Digital Library of Romania.
4. Build individual records for each archaeological site rather than merging them into monument ensembles.
5. Map excavation locations and research years.
6. Record finds and stratigraphic observations separately from later interpretations.
7. Investigate prehistoric and ancient material with exact provenance before using it to reconstruct settlement continuity.
8. Research archaeological evidence outside the protected historic centre.

## Current conclusion

The archaeological evidence already shows that the historical archive must operate at multiple spatial scales:

**city territory → historic urban site → monument/ensemble → excavation area → trench/context → find/publication**

That hierarchy will become part of the future data model.
